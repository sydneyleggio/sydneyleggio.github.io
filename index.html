CosmoLab
Detecting SGWB Using Astrometry
Gravitational waves leave a quadrupolar imprint on the proper motions of stars across the sky. I am developing the angular power spectrum formalism to extract this signal, building toy models to derive optimal estimators for the astrometric setting, and characterizing what information real surveys like the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope can realistically recover once survey geometry and noise are taken into account.
UltraViolet EXplorer
UVEX Gravitational Wave Follow-Up
Optimized electromagnetic follow-up strategies for gravitational wave-detected binary neutron star mergers by simulating observing scenarios tailored to UVEX's capabilities. These simulations targeted kilonova light curves within critical detection windows. Statistical modeling of survey efficiency and cadence connected telescope design choices to maximized follow-up prospects for transient astrophysical events, with simulations extended to the upcoming ULTRASAT mission.

Technical & Quantitative Work

A selection of technical projects and outcomes spanning large-scale simulation, real-time systems, and predictive modeling.

Signal Processing · HPC
Large-Scale SNR Simulation & Memory Optimization
Diagnosed a critical memory bottleneck in a signal-to-noise-ratio simulation for gravitational-wave astrometry. The original implementation had a peak allocation of about 870 GB and failed at production scale. Redesigned the core algorithm to stream calculations incrementally, cutting peak memory to about 75 MB while validating bit-for-bit numerical equivalence against the original implementation. Independently formulate and test hypotheses on signal behavior across varying survey geometries and noise models, running large-scale simulations on USC's CARC Discovery HPC cluster.
Real-Time Systems · Predictive Modeling
TURBO Observatory: Low-Latency Detection & Tracking Pipeline
Engineered low-latency Python pipelines for real-time detection and tracking of objects in under 30 seconds. Designed dynamic algorithms for automated identification and tracking using predictive modeling and feature extraction, with an improved GitHub-based workflow for debugging and reliability.
C++ · Orbital Mechanics
Predictive Modeling for CubeSat Orbital Mechanics
Developed C++ algorithms for predictive modeling and error correction in orbital mechanics for the IMPRESS and EXACT CubeSats, two NSF- and AFRL-funded missions. Contributed to ADCS hardware testing and calibration before moving into system-level project management for both missions.

I am a graduate researcher in physics at the University of Southern California, advised by Dr. Kris Pardo, working at the intersection of precision astrometry, gravitational-wave cosmology, and large-scale statistical modeling.

My research focuses on developing the angular power spectrum formalism for detecting a stochastic gravitational-wave background through stellar astrometry. I also characterize what information real surveys like the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope can realistically extract given survey geometry and noise. This work has required rebuilding simulation code to run efficiently at scale on HPC clusters.

Before USC, I completed my B.S. in Astrophysics and Physics at the University of Minnesota, where I managed two NSF- and AFRL-funded CubeSat missions, built real-time detection pipelines for the TURBO Observatory, and completed my senior thesis on optimizing electromagnetic follow-up strategies for gravitational-wave events using the UltraViolet EXplorer (UVEX).

Outside formal research, I focus on the quantitative and computational side of physics. This includes predictive modeling, optimization, and building systems that handle data at scale.
